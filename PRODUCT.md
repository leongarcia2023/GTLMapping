# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

GTLMapping serves infrared astronomy researchers who want to make, inspect,
and compare mid infrared extinction maps. They need enough methodological
detail to reproduce a result, choose a foreground profile, and understand
which pixels are detections or censored lower limits.

## Product Purpose

The package and documentation provide one transparent interface for the BT12
constant foreground method and three spatial foreground profiles. Success
means that a researcher can trace every map to its image, foreground,
background, opacity, mask policy, and uncertainty inputs without relying on
private project history.

## Brand Personality

Rigorous, candid, readable. The site should feel like a careful methods paper
with executable examples, not a sales page or a laboratory notebook.

## Anti-references

Avoid private correspondence, manuscript workflow, personal attribution for
unpublished inputs, internal release notes, AI sounding transitions, and
decorative punctuation. Do not overstate physical validation or use marketing
language to hide a scientific limitation.

## Design Principles

1. Show the calculation with real data before asking readers to trust it.
2. Separate measured quantities, model choices, and sensitivity tests.
3. Put BT12 and GTL on the same background, opacity, aperture, and mask when
   comparing them.
4. Mark censored values and constraints visibly instead of converting them to
   ordinary detections or unexplained missing pixels.
5. Write for public reuse. Every claim should stand without private context.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA contrast and keyboard navigation. Figures must include
descriptive alternative text, must not rely on color alone, and must remain
legible on narrow screens. Respect reduced motion; the documentation does not
need animation to explain the method.
